The Unraveling of August Finch

The Athenaeum Files · Book One

Some legacies are buried. Others are left as clues.

When charismatic museum archivist August Finch vanishes during a blackout at the Boston Athenaeum, he leaves behind a clean desk, a locked safe, and a single page from a nineteenth-century ledger that shouldn’t exist. His estranged daughter, Maya — a forensic accountant who deals exclusively in hard numbers — is forced to step into her father’s world of missing artifacts and polished secrets.

To track him down, she must follow a trail of meticulously staged historical anomalies left specifically for her, forcing her to calculate the exact cost of the lies that held her family together.

An excerpt

Chapter One

The safe was already open when I arrived, which was the first thing that was wrong.

My father did not leave things open. He was a man who closed drawers with his hip while carrying two boxes, who put the cap back on a pen between sentences. Twenty-two years of not speaking to him had not changed the shape of him in my head, and the shape did not include an open safe.

Inside: nothing. Not emptied — arranged. A felt liner brushed flat. Four shallow indentations where four objects had sat long enough to leave a mark, and no objects.

On the desk, squared to the blotter with an accountant’s precision he had no business having, was a single page of ruled paper. Nineteenth century. Iron gall ink. The columns drawn by hand, the way they were before anyone thought to print them.

I know what that page was worth. I know to the dollar what everything in that room was worth, because that is what I do, and because he knew it too.

He had left me a document. Of course he had. It was the only language we still had in common.
